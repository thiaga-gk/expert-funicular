The Publisher prices in three layers. The candidate estimator prices one change as if it were the only one. The {{term:stateful-bundle}} prices a scenario step by step, each step acting on what the previous steps left. The package reports an {{term:envelope}} over the scenarios. All three use the same {{term:low-base-high}} convention, keep {{term:dbu}} and {{term:aws-cost}} apart, and publish null, never zero, for a cost they cannot know.

Three tracks, and why they cross

Every modeled cost is computed on three tracks, conservative, base and optimistic. They differ in how much of a modeled effect actually shows up: the realization factors {{fact:thresholds.realization_conservative}}, {{fact:thresholds.realization_base}} and {{fact:thresholds.realization_optimistic}} (realization_conservative, realization_base, realization_optimistic). The optimistic track has the lowest cost and therefore the highest savings, so the ranges cross: savings low pairs with cost high.

Signed savings from a cost range{{cite:src/publisher/optimizer/estimator/savings.py::SavingsModel|SavingsModel._delta_range}}
savings.low = baseline − cost.high (conservative track) savings.base = baseline − cost.base savings.high = baseline − cost.low (optimistic track)
auto-stop, DBU, ge12: low = {{ex:autostop.baseline_dbu}} − {{ex:autostop.cost_high}} = {{pkg:candidates[knob=auto_stop_minutes].independent_economics.savings_365d.dbu.low|money}} ✓ base = {{ex:autostop.baseline_dbu}} − {{ex:autostop.cost_base}} = {{pkg:candidates[knob=auto_stop_minutes].independent_economics.savings_365d.dbu.base|money}} ✓ high = {{ex:autostop.baseline_dbu}} − {{ex:autostop.cost_low}} = {{pkg:candidates[knob=auto_stop_minutes].independent_economics.savings_365d.dbu.high|money}} ✓
Savings are signed: a negative value is a cost increase, which happens on purpose for a capacity increase and by accident on a pessimistic track. The total is DBU plus AWS on each track, and becomes null when either side is unknown.
{{range:pkg|candidates[knob=auto_stop_minutes].independent_economics.savings_365d.total|Auto-stop 45 → 10, isolated total savings}}
Not a probability interval

Low and high are what the model yields when the effect realizes at the conservative or the optimistic rate. They are scenario bounds, not percentiles of a distribution. Unknown is null, never 0.

The forward baseline

Every saving is measured against the {{term:baseline}}: what the warehouse would cost over the next 365 days with no change. {{cite:src/publisher/analyzer/forecast.py::Modeler|Modeler.forecast_from_daily_cost}} builds it from the current epoch's settled, watermark-covered billing days only.

Run-rate forecast{{cite:src/publisher/analyzer/metrics.py::blended_daily_rate|metrics.py}}
rate = the daily cost series cut to whole weeks (no weekday/weekend bias) daily = w × mean(last forecast_recent_days) + (1 − w) × mean(last forecast_long_days) w = forecast_weight_28_long with a full long window, forecast_weight_28_mid with at least the recent window ratio = clamp( mean(last 28) / mean(previous 28), trend_ratio_floor, trend_ratio_ceiling ) trend = 1 + trend_adjustment_weight × (ratio − 1) forecast_365 = daily × trend × 365
windows {{fact:thresholds.forecast_recent_days}} and {{fact:thresholds.forecast_long_days}} days w = {{fact:thresholds.forecast_weight_28_long}} or {{fact:thresholds.forecast_weight_28_mid}} ratio clamped to [{{fact:thresholds.trend_ratio_floor}}, {{fact:thresholds.trend_ratio_ceiling}}] trend weight {{fact:thresholds.trend_adjustment_weight}} ge12 forward cost: DBU {{pkg:cost_baseline.forward_365d.dbu_cost|money}} + AWS {{pkg:cost_baseline.forward_365d.aws_cost|money}} = {{pkg:cost_baseline.forward_365d.total_cost|money}} {{pkg:cost_baseline.currency}}
The trend is damped twice: the ratio is clamped, then only trend_adjustment_weight of the change moves the forecast. A warehouse that doubled last month is not projected to double again.

Periodic workloads, such as a month-end close, would look like noise to a run rate. {{cite:src/publisher/analyzer/forecast.py::billing_cadence}} detects short runs (billed days at most {{fact:thresholds.forecast_run_bridge_days}} days apart belong to one run) resting longer than {{fact:thresholds.forecast_cadence_gap_days}} days, and then uses the cadence model: the cost of whole run-to-run cycles divided by their days, times 365, with no trend. It needs at least {{fact:thresholds.forecast_min_cycles}} whole cycles.

When the forecast cannot be known it is null, and dbu_forecast_status says why: UNPRICED_USAGE (a DBU row with no list price), INSUFFICIENT_ACTIVE_COST_DAYS (fewer than {{fact:thresholds.min_economics_cost_history_days}} billed days), NON_POSITIVE_RUN_RATE or INSUFFICIENT_BILLING_CYCLES. Every candidate estimate scales this baseline, so a null baseline withholds them all. The AWS forward cost is modeled from running cluster time with the same blend, so DBU and AWS describe the same year.

Candidate estimator: one change at a time

{{cite:src/publisher/optimizer/estimator/candidate.py::Estimator}} turns each decision into cost factors on the forward DBU baseline, one per track, in {{cite:src/publisher/optimizer/estimator/cost.py::CostModel|CostModel._factors}}. A factor below 1 is a saving. Each candidate is priced against the same baseline as if it were alone, which is why candidate savings are {{term:non-additive}} and carry candidate_savings_must_not_be_summed.

Auto-stop

The reclaimable running cluster-minutes per day at the target value, as a share of today's running cluster-minutes. Only the idle a lower value removes is scaled by realization; restarts count in full on every track.

share_r = min(1, realized × r + fixed), factor_r = max(0, 1 − share_r). Lower: realized = gain, fixed = −loss. Raise: realized = 0, fixed = gain − loss.

Min clusters

Spare floor capacity from {{cite:src/publisher/analyzer/metrics.py::floor_spare_shares}}: each running minute at the floor whose demand needed d clusters holds floor − max(k, d) clusters a floor of k would remove, with d = ceil((running + queued) / QUERIES_PER_CLUSTER_PRO_CLASSIC).

factor_r = max(0, 1 − spare_share × r). Spare and running minutes are annualized the same way, so the trend cancels.

Size

factor_r = capacity_ratio × (busy_share × multiplier_r + 1 − busy_share), clamped to [{{fact:thresholds.cost_factor_floor}}, {{fact:thresholds.cost_factor_ceiling}}]. A downsize slows busy time by {{fact:thresholds.downsize_runtime_multiplier_optimistic}}, {{fact:thresholds.downsize_runtime_multiplier_base}} or {{fact:thresholds.downsize_runtime_multiplier_conservative}}; idle time does not change.

The capacity ratio is the published Serverless DBU per hour, or for Pro/Classic the workspace's own billed rates, accepted only within {{fact:thresholds.size_rate_published_band}} of the published size ratio ({{cite:src/publisher/optimizer/estimator/cost.py::validated_size_rate_ratio}}). Otherwise the estimate is withheld (PRO_CLASSIC_SIZE_RATE_UNVERIFIED).

Max-cluster increase

Optimistic adds nothing: the same queries finish on more clusters at once. Worst case: every added cluster runs whenever the warehouse sat at its ceiling, raw = minutes_at_max × Δclusters / cluster_minutes. Base for Pro/Classic: only the clusters each busy minute's demand needs under the new ceiling, from the concurrency histogram.

factor = (1, 1 + base, 1 + raw). A ceiling cut is a guardrail with factor 1.

Migration

{{cite:src/publisher/optimizer/estimator/cost.py::CostModel|CostModel._migration_costs}} prices the target type at the current size, running at its own best auto-stop: DBU = target cluster-minutes / 60 × DBU per hour × unit price ÷ reconciliation.

The same formula must reproduce today's billed forecast within [{{fact:thresholds.migration_reconciliation_min}}, {{fact:thresholds.migration_reconciliation_max}}] (MIGRATION_MODEL_NOT_RECONCILED otherwise), so systematic model error cancels. Serverless AWS is a true zero: its infrastructure is inside the DBU line.

No change, blocked, unknown

NO_CHANGE and the max-cluster guardrail have factor 1, so their savings are a real 0. BLOCKED, INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE, a rollback, a missing baseline or an unvalidated rate have no factor: the economics are null and estimate_blocker names why.

AWS reuses the DBU factors for every knob except size, where it uses the EC2 cluster-hour ratio times the runtime multiplier ({{cite:src/publisher/optimizer/estimator/cost.py::CostModel|_aws_factor_range}}).

The other ge12 candidates show the per-knob rules at work. The size canary's isolated total savings run from {{pkg:candidates[knob=warehouse_size].independent_economics.savings_365d.total.low|money}} to {{pkg:candidates[knob=warehouse_size].independent_economics.savings_365d.total.high|money}}: in this example, the slowdown on busy time on the conservative track almost exactly cancels the cheaper rate. Confidence

Each candidate carries a deterministic {{term:confidence}} score from {{cite:src/publisher/optimizer/estimator/candidate.py::Estimator|Estimator._confidence}}. Unknown inputs earn no credit, and the score is capped by status, so a canary can never look as certain as an observed change.

Candidate confidence{{doc:docs/architecture/publisher/lld.md|LLD §16}}
score = clamp( w_base + w_cov × coverage + w_sample × sample + w_stab × stability + w_epoch × epoch_maturity + w_signal × signal, 0, cap(status) ) coverage = cost history days / confidence_coverage_days epoch_maturity = epoch age / confidence_epoch_maturity_days sample = the knob's decision sample / its full-credit size stability = 1 − cv84 / 2 level = HIGH ≥ confidence_high_threshold, MEDIUM ≥ confidence_medium_threshold, else LOW
weights: base {{ex:weights.base}} coverage {{ex:weights.coverage}} sample {{ex:weights.sample}} stability {{ex:weights.stability}} epoch {{ex:weights.epoch}} signal {{ex:weights.signal}} auto-stop ({{ex:confidence_autostop.status}}): weighted sum {{ex:confidence_autostop.raw}} → cap {{ex:confidence_autostop.cap}} → {{ex:confidence_autostop.score}} {{ex:confidence_autostop.level}} ✓ size ({{ex:confidence_size.status}}): weighted sum {{ex:confidence_size.raw}} → cap {{ex:confidence_size.cap}} → {{ex:confidence_size.score}} {{ex:confidence_size.level}} ✓
Both candidates have near-perfect evidence (stability {{ex:confidence_autostop.stability}}, every other component 1.000), so the status cap alone separates them. The caps sit below the level cut-offs on purpose: a TEST_REQUIRED change stays MEDIUM until its canary is measured.

The decision sample differs per knob (executed queries for size and auto-stop, busy minutes for max clusters, busy periods for min clusters), set in _DECISION_SAMPLE. A decision that changes nothing earns a lower signal credit (confidence_signal_no_change). A scenario's confidence is the minimum of its steps' scores ({{cite:src/publisher/packager/scenarios.py::ScenarioPlanner|ScenarioPlanner._scenario_confidence}}): a plan is as trustworthy as its weakest step.

Scenarios: the stateful bundle estimator

Two changes to one warehouse overlap. Auto-stop and a lower floor both remove running cluster-minutes, and a minute cannot be saved twice. {{cite:src/publisher/packager/estimator/scenario.py::StatefulBundleEstimator}} therefore walks the plan in order, carrying a state of DBU, AWS and resource tracks ({{cite:src/publisher/packager/estimator/state_transition.py::_ChainState}}), and applies each candidate's bundle_effect to what the previous steps left.

idle_cluster_minute_reduction

Auto-stop. Removes (realized × r + fixed) × today's running minutes from the resource track, less any overlap with an earlier floor cut, and scales DBU and AWS by the share removed.

min_cluster_reduction

A lower floor. Claims the spare floor capacity minus the wall-clock minutes an earlier auto-stop already removed per floor cluster, realized per track. The floor track keeps the part of the cut it did not realize.

multiplicative_cost_factor

Size or a ceiling increase. The runtime part moves running time by a fixed amount, after = before + acted × (rf − 1); the price part (factor / rf) applies as a ratio. This keeps the total independent of step order.

Three more effects complete the set: migration_cost_override replaces the state with the migration's pre-canary costs; requires_canary and estimate_withheld halt the chain, so every later figure is null; no_cost_change does nothing.

Plan order

{{cite:src/publisher/packager/sequencing.py::PlanOrdering|PlanOrdering._plan_order}} ranks steps by value per unit of risk, then moves each step behind every step that invalidates it.

Step value{{cite:src/publisher/packager/sequencing.py::PlanOrdering|sequencing.py}}
value = base savings × confidence / risk risk = plan_risk_canary_change for TEST_REQUIRED, plan_risk_hitl_change otherwise order = canary rollbacks → priced positive steps by value → unpriced steps → zero or negative steps ties by knob rank; steps an earlier step invalidates go last
ge12: auto-stop {{pkg:candidates[knob=auto_stop_minutes].independent_economics.savings_365d.total.base|money}} × {{pkg:candidates[knob=auto_stop_minutes].confidence.score}} / {{fact:thresholds.plan_risk_hitl_change}} size {{pkg:candidates[knob=warehouse_size].independent_economics.savings_365d.total.base|money}} × {{pkg:candidates[knob=warehouse_size].confidence.score}} / {{fact:thresholds.plan_risk_canary_change}} → auto-stop goes first
A canary costs a measurement cycle and may roll back, so its value is divided by the larger risk. Execution order adds one more rule: approval-only steps go first and canaries start once those have run long enough, so a canary's cost reading is not mixed with another change's saving.

The ge12 plan says so in its decision summary: "{{pkg:recommended_plan.decision_summary}}"

Package economics: an envelope

Scenarios are alternatives, so their savings must not be added either. {{cite:src/publisher/packager/estimator/package.py::_package_economics}} reports the spread across the priced scenarios instead.

Package savings envelope{{doc:docs/contracts/shared/reporter-json-contract.md|Reporter contract §15}}
low = min over priced scenarios of savings.total.low base = the recommended scenario's savings.total.base high = max over priced scenarios of savings.total.high cost = total baseline − savings (same envelope, inverted)
low {{ex:envelope.low}} base {{ex:envelope.base}} high {{ex:envelope.high}} ✓
Only scenarios with an authoritative total price are included, and only when the recommended scenario itself is priced; otherwise the package savings are null and each scenario is listed with an exclusion reason (SCENARIO_ECONOMICS_UNAVAILABLE, RECOMMENDED_SCENARIO_ECONOMICS_UNAVAILABLE, NO_RECOMMENDED_SCENARIO).

The auto-stop candidate saves {{pkg:candidates[knob=auto_stop_minutes].independent_economics.savings_365d.total.base|money}} and the size candidate {{pkg:candidates[knob=warehouse_size].independent_economics.savings_365d.total.base|money}} at base. Why is the plan's base not their sum?

Each candidate is priced alone against the full baseline. In the plan, the size step acts on the running time the auto-stop step left, so its own saving is smaller, as the plan table above shows. Only the stateful bundle may combine steps.

Why does savings low pair with cost high?

Low savings is the conservative track, where the modeled effect realizes least, so the cost after the change is highest. _delta_range pairs them so that low ≤ base ≤ high always holds.

Both ge12 canaries have near-perfect evidence. Why is the size canary MEDIUM?

Its status caps the score at confidence_cap_test_required, below the HIGH cut-off. Confidence describes how far the evidence can be trusted for this kind of change, and a modeled resize has not been observed yet.

The forecast is null. What happens to the candidates?

Every candidate factor scales the forward DBU baseline, so all estimates are withheld with an estimate_blocker naming the baseline's reason. An ACTIONABLE cost change then waits for its price at the value gate.
